# Game Design

## Foundation loop

1. Create or join a room with a five-character code.
2. Four to eight witnesses mark ready.
3. The host begins the investigation.
4. Witnesses move through the Orpheus Research Complex.
5. Proximity reveals inspectable objects.
6. Inspection produces an authored observation or a verified physical record. Some recorders require another witness's badge.
7. Share selected memories, stake confidence, challenge claims, and compare conflicting details.
8. Reconstruct six events from public sources. Three physical records and a connected majority lock the theory.
9. Watch six room-by-room comparisons, then review confidence, belief propagation, awards, and group archetype.
10. Rematch with changed facts, or share the finished case and challenge another group.

The world is the primary interface. Overlays are brief and return focus to movement.

## Map intent

The Blackout is arranged around a readable Central Atrium. Three labs create the technical north wing. The Main Hall and Emergency Exit create a strong west-east route. Storage, Security, CCTV, Server, and Archive form a dense investigation wing to the south.

Each room has distinct geometry, lighting, and fixture silhouettes. Critical paths cross the atrium so players repeatedly encounter one another.

## Interaction cadence

Twelve objects are distributed across the facility. Eight recorders bind to six canonical events, including corroborating sources. Other objects provide atmosphere. Badge clearances divide records across four groups, making cross-checking necessary. No witness receives every event in their journal.

## Solo rehearsal

Three labelled scripted witnesses walk actual collision-constrained paths, recover badge-authorized records, share false and true memories, form a false consensus, change a belief, propose a reconstruction, and vote. They use the same engine as humans. Contextual messages introduce a new decision or discovery every twenty to thirty seconds. The human can overwrite their proposal and vote sooner. Automatic lock occurs around three minutes, followed by a twenty-six-second reveal. The clock pauses with no human connected.
