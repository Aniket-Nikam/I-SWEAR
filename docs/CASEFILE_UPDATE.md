# Casefile gameplay update

New rooms use a versioned casefile ruleset. Existing saved rooms retain their original rules until rematch. The immutable incident, server-owned movement, private memories, badge access and confidence stakes remain the foundation.

- Records expose complementary facts. Two independent records can establish an event; a testimony can fill gaps but remains unverified. Linking sources never imports facts the group has not recovered or shared. A withdrawn testimony must be removed from a deduction before final voting.
- Each witness receives a private pre-incident motive and an optional cooperative objective. They decide when to disclose it. Motives never supply hidden incident answers and never change the team's truth score. Private payloads are sent only to their authenticated owner; all motives become visible after the reveal.
- Multiplayer rounds run for six active minutes: search, first-theory discussion, a new corroborating lead, renewed search, final call. Solo rehearsals of about three minutes use a shorter clock. The first theory can be recorded by a majority and otherwise gets a provisional checkpoint. The timer pauses when no human is connected. Final majority voting becomes available with the new lead; the deadline can close an incomplete record.
- The reveal animates a small SVG reconstruction, compares the first theory with the final record, and explains plausible origins of distorted memories. It does not replay or fabricate new incident facts.
- Reliable actions are ordered under a per-room lock. Bounded socket writers coalesce obsolete position/clock messages, merging movement deltas per witness, preserving reliable events and preventing a slow recipient from blocking the simulation. A single coalesced checkpoint writer keeps database latency out of each socket's movement-reading path. Replay and account stats commit once per case; completed replays reconcile stale room checkpoints after a crash. New gameplay events stay outside the per-frame render loop. Static map textures and local movement prediction are preserved.

Verification must cover private-data boundaries, partial facts and link conflicts, stale revisions, retraction invalidation, deadline/majority races, disconnected clock pause, checkpoint restore, rematch reset, four/eight-client play, bounded outbound queues, client controls and reduced-motion replay. A final public round is checked after deployment. This work cannot promise the absence of every possible bug; the release is gated on those checks.
